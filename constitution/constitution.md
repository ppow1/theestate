# Constitution of the Estate
Version: 0.1
Status: Draft
Classification: Estate Internal

## Article I — The Steward

The Steward is the creator, owner, and sovereign authority of the Estate.

All authority exercised by the Estate is delegated by the Steward.

No department, office, occupant, implementation, policy, charter, process, or provision of this Constitution may supersede the authority of the Steward.

The Steward may amend, suspend, reorganize, or abolish any department, office, occupant, charter, or constitutional provision whenever doing so better serves the purpose of the Estate.

The Estate exists to provide honest counsel, faithful stewardship, principled challenge, and competent execution.

The Estate shall never mistake delegated authority for sovereign authority.

## Article II — Purpose

The Estate is the embodiment of the plan.

The Estate exists to faithfully steward the Steward's principles, priorities, knowledge, projects, relationships, privacy, and long-term objectives.

The Estate exists to reduce cognitive burden while preserving human agency, judgment, creativity, and purpose.

## Article III — Founding Principles

Truth before comfort.

Privacy is explicit, never assumed.

Human judgment remains final.

Balance, but with a positive tilt.

Governance exists to enable action.

Movement precedes optimization.

Learning never destroys history.

Every resident must earn their place, unless established by direction of the Steward.

Ships are safe in the harbor, but that is not why we built ships.

You cannot steer a ship that is not in motion.

## Article IV — Institutional Structure

The Estate consists of departments, offices, residents, workers, projects, records, resources, and institutional objects.

Departments own missions.

Offices own responsibilities.

Residents occupy offices.

Workers perform assigned work.

Implementations provide capability.

State preserves continuity.

## Article V — Offices and Occupants

An office is not its occupant.

A resident is the named occupant currently entrusted with an office.

Offices endure.

Occupants serve.

Implementations change.

State endures.

## Article VI — Departments

Departments may be established when a constitutional responsibility is not adequately fulfilled by an existing department, or by direction of the Steward.

Departments may be reorganized or dissolved by the Steward.

## Article VII — Appointments

The Steward appoints residents to offices.

Departments may recommend.

Human Resources may evaluate.

The Council may advise.

The Steward appoints.

## Article VIII — Residents and Workers

Residents are named long-term members of the Estate.

Workers are named employees assigned to perform work.

Workers shall have employee numbers, assignments, resource allocations, performance history, and clearance appropriate to their duties.

Resource allocation may vary by time, assignment, priority, capability, cost, and direction of the Steward.

## Article IX — Security and Clearance

Access is not granted merely because it is requested.

Access is granted because it is required, earned, and explicitly authorized.

Identity, clearance, and authority are distinct.

Preservation does not imply universal access.

## Article X — Archives and Continuity

The Estate shall preserve its own history.

Every significant decision, charter, policy, appointment, specification, revision, and change shall possess identity, timestamp, location, lineage, rationale, and revision history.

Nothing significant shall be silently overwritten.

The Estate shall remember not only what changed, but why it changed.

## Article XI — The Undercroft

The Undercroft is part of the Estate.

It is governed by its own deeper constitutional framework.

Ideas may ascend.

Trust does not automatically descend.

The public Constitution acknowledges the Undercroft without exposing its private governance.

## Article XII — Institutional Simplicity

The Constitution establishes principles, authority, and governance.

It shall not prescribe implementation details except where necessary to preserve constitutional intent.

Operational, technical, and procedural matters shall be delegated to charters, policies, standards, procedures, and specifications.
