WP-4111 establishes the Estate’s canonical method for recording, generating, comparing, and validating resident height, proportions, and scale across portraits, full-body references, turnarounds, environmental scenes, seated images, and group photography. It addresses distortions caused by camera angle, focal length, framing, footwear, pose, furniture scale, and inconsistent body geometry.

The work package requires canonical height in metric and imperial units, measurable proportion landmarks, repeatable camera and framing controls, flat and heeled comparison standards, environmental references, posture and gait notes, vertically aligned comparison sheets, relative-height validation in group scenes, and explicit regeneration requirements when scale checks fail.

# 1. Canonical Purpose

The Height, Proportion, and Scale Reference System exists to ensure that a resident remains physically consistent when shown:

- 

alone;

- 

seated;

- 

standing;

- 

walking;

- 

wearing flat footwear;

- 

wearing heels;

- 

beside furniture;

- 

beside a doorway;

- 

behind a desk;

- 

beside other residents;

- 

at different camera distances;

- 

under different approved lenses;

- 

in portrait, full-body, turnaround, and group formats.

The system must prevent a resident from appearing:

- 

correctly proportioned in a headshot but incorrect in full-body imagery;

- 

shorter or taller because of uncontrolled perspective;

- 

broader or narrower because of lens distortion;

- 

canonically taller because of footwear;

- 

mis-scaled against furniture;

- 

mis-scaled against another resident;

- 

differently proportioned between front and profile views;

- 

vertically misaligned in comparison sheets;

- 

silently accepted despite a material scale failure.

# 2. Governing Principles

## 2.1 True height, apparent height, and presentation height are separate

The system must preserve three distinct concepts:

### Canonical true height

The resident’s governed standing height, measured under the canonical baseline condition.

### Apparent height

How tall the resident appears in a specific image because of:

- 

camera position;

- 

focal length;

- 

crop;

- 

perspective;

- 

posture;

- 

pose;

- 

environment;

- 

occlusion;

- 

viewer interpretation.

### Footwear-adjusted presentation height

The effective vertical presentation when approved footwear is worn.

These values must never be collapsed into one field.

## 2.2 Scale is relational and contextual

Height consistency cannot be validated from a face crop or isolated portrait alone.

Reliable scale review requires one or more of:

- 

calibrated full-body framing;

- 

known environmental objects;

- 

standardized furniture;

- 

known doorway dimensions;

- 

controlled camera geometry;

- 

a canonically sized comparison resident;

- 

anatomical landmark alignment.

## 2.3 Camera geometry is part of the evidence

A scale-sensitive asset is incomplete without its camera conditions.

The Estate must record:

- 

focal length or equivalent field of view;

- 

sensor or rendering frame assumptions where relevant;

- 

camera height;

- 

camera pitch;

- 

camera roll;

- 

camera-to-subject distance;

- 

horizon line;

- 

subject distance;

- 

crop;

- 

canvas dimensions;

- 

perspective correction;

- 

lens distortion correction.

## 2.4 Footwear does not rewrite the body

Footwear may change:

- 

total presentation height;

- 

ankle position;

- 

pelvic tilt;

- 

knee posture;

- 

stride;

- 

apparent leg length;

- 

stance;

- 

silhouette.

Footwear may not alter:

- 

canonical standing height;

- 

canonical inseam;

- 

canonical body proportions;

- 

resident identity.

## 2.5 Posture is not height

Slouching, head tilt, hip shift, bent knees, contrapposto, and footwear-induced posture must not be mistaken for changes in canonical stature.

## 2.6 Relative-height ordering is canonical evidence

In multi-resident imagery, the approved relative-height order must remain visible and plausible.

Captions cannot rescue an image whose perspective makes resident scale ambiguous.

## 2.7 Comparison imagery must minimize eye travel

Variants intended for direct comparison must be vertically aligned so equivalent anatomical landmarks occupy the same image coordinates wherever practical.

## 2.8 Failures require regeneration, not silent tolerance

A materially incorrect scale asset must:

- 

fail review;

- 

receive a specific defect code;

- 

identify the likely cause;

- 

produce a bounded regeneration instruction;

- 

remain noncanonical.
