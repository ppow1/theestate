 the doorway.

Expected:

- 

doorway comparison is invalid;

- 

no height conclusion is drawn;

- 

regeneration aligns depth or applies calibrated correction.

## Scenario E — Group order reversed

A shorter resident appears taller because she is closer to the camera.

Expected:

- 

neutral group scale validation fails;

- 

scene may remain noncanonical artistic imagery;

- 

canonical group proof requires controlled depth.

## Scenario F — Seated mismatch

The resident’s feet cannot reach the floor in one image but do in another using the same chair.

Expected:

- 

inconsistency is flagged;

- 

chair dimensions and body proportions are reviewed;

- 

at least one asset is regenerated.

## Scenario G — Vertical sheet misalignment

Three variants use different crops and floor lines.

Expected:

- 

sheet fails;

- 

panels are regenerated or reassembled using identical geometry;

- 

reviewers are not required to compare separate windows.

## Scenario H — Resident-specific inapplicability

A resident’s canon does not support high heels.

Expected:

- 

heel comparison is marked not applicable with rationale;

- 

neutral footwear remains the scale baseline;

- 

no forced wardrobe contradiction is introduced.

# 40. Anti-Patterns

## 40.1 Height by caption

Bad:

```

```
She is 4'10"
```

```

under an image that visually depicts average height.

## 40.2 Independent group resizing

Bad:

- 

scaling one resident layer until the composition looks balanced.

## 40.3 Furniture as vague proof

Bad:

- 

“She looks short next to a chair,” when the chair size is unknown.

## 40.4 Cropped scale reference

Bad:

- 

missing floor contact or top of head.

## 40.5 Mixed camera conditions

Bad:

- 

comparing variants with different focal lengths, distances, and horizon lines.

## 40.6 Heel contamination

Bad:

- 

storing footwear-adjusted height as canonical height.

## 40.7 Pose as correction

Bad:

- 

bending a taller resident or stretching a shorter resident to preserve order.

## 40.8 Forced landmark alignment

Bad:

- 

aligning all waist and knee landmarks when those are the actual tested variables.

## 40.9 Silent acceptance

Bad:

- 

retaining a materially wrong scale asset because the face looks good.

## 40.10 Uncontrolled group perspective

Bad:

- 

judging relative height when residents occupy materially different depth planes.

# 42. Completion Criteria

WP-4111 may be considered implementation complete only when:

- 

true height, apparent height, and presentation height are distinct;

- 

canonical height supports metric and imperial units;

- 

head-to-body ratio is represented;

- 

shoulder-to-waist proportion is represented;

- 

waist-to-floor proportion is represented;

- 

inseam, torso, leg, and arm descriptors are represented;

- 

posture baseline is represented;

- 

stance and gait notes are represented;

- 

camera and lens metadata are mandatory for scale-sensitive assets;

- 

reusable camera presets exist;

- 

flat or minimal-footwear references exist;

- 

applicable 3-inch-or-greater heel references exist;

- 

footwear never overwrites canonical height;

- 

front, profile, rear, and three-quarter references exist;

- 

a standardized seated reference exists;

- 

a doorway or desk reference exists;

- 

environmental dimensions are recorded;

- 

pair or group scale references exist;

- 

relative-height ordering is machine-readable;

- 

captions are not accepted as scale proof;

- 

stacked comparison sheets align controlled landmarks;

- 

failed scale checks produce regeneration requests;

- 

every canonical scale-sensitive asset records camera and footwear conditions;

- 

full-body, seated, environmental, and group representations remain consistent;

- 

provenance and audit history are complete;

- 

privacy rules are enforced;

- 

unit tests pass;

- 

integration tests pass;

- 

validation scenarios pass;

operator documentation is complete.
