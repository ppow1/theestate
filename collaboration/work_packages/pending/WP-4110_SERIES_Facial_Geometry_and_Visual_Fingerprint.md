# WP-4110 — Facial Geometry and Visual Fingerprint

Status: Pending
Series: Visual Estate Initiative
Campaign: 01 — Visual Bible Foundation
Depends on: WP-4108, WP-4109

## Objective

Define the facial-identity and visual-fingerprint standard that lets each resident remain recognizable across photorealism, black-and-white studies, icons, thumbnails, wardrobe changes, lighting changes, and group scenes.

## Scope

For each adult resident, define:

- face shape and proportions
- brow line and brow behavior
- eye shape, spacing, color, lid structure, gaze character, and catchlight preferences
- nose, cheek, jaw, chin, lip, smile, and ear landmarks
- hairline, texture, length, volume, movement, and approved styling range
- apparent age range and maturity cues
- signature head angle, posture, expression, and emotional presence
- visual identifiers that must survive downscaling to icon size
- ambiguity notes that describe artistic impression without asserting ancestry from appearance
- forbidden drift patterns and negative-generation guidance

## Visual Fingerprint

A resident's visual fingerprint is the minimum combination of stable cues required for recognition when name, title, wardrobe, and background are removed.

It must include:

1. three to seven primary recognition cues;
2. secondary cues that may vary without breaking identity;
3. cues that must never be exaggerated into caricature;
4. icon-scale cues;
5. group-photo differentiation cues.

## Evelyn Example Direction

Capture, as provisional Steward notes:

- warm hazel eyes;
- soft brunette hair with natural movement;
- slight attentive head tilt;
- gentle, reassuring smile;
- approachable mature-professional presence;
- rare facial ambiguity so subtle that only a few observers out of one hundred might briefly wonder about an East Asian influence, without any definitive ethnic reading or ancestry claim.

This is an artistic-intent note, not a demographic classification.

## Deliverables

1. Facial-geometry schema.
2. Visual-fingerprint schema.
3. Human-readable authoring guide.
4. Thumbnail-recognition requirements.
5. Cross-style identity-preservation rules.
6. Evelyn example and migration template for all residents.

## Acceptance Criteria

- A resident can be recognized without title, office, or signature wardrobe.
- The same identity survives photorealistic, monochrome, sketch, and thumbnail treatments.
- Group imagery does not collapse residents into variations of one generic face.
- Artistic ambiguity is recorded without claiming ethnicity from visual appearance.
- Tests reject missing primary cues, contradictory facial landmarks, and unscoped identity overrides.
